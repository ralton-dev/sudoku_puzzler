The Hidden Pair and Hidden Triple at size four — and, like its naked twin, a
technique this ladder never has to use.

## The rule

Take a unit and count places per digit. If four digits are all confined to the
*same* four squares, those four digits fill those four squares between them, one
each. Every other candidate in those four squares can be erased. A digit may
reach only two or three of the four squares; what matters is that none of the
four leaks outside.

## A worked example

In box 3 the digits 2, 5, 7 and 8 can go only in r1c7, r1c9, r3c7 and r3c9 —
each digit reaching two or three of them, none reaching anywhere else in the
box. Four digits, four squares. So those squares take a 2, a 5, a 7 and an 8
between them, and any 1, 3, 4, 6 or 9 pencilled into them can go, even though
the four squares looked crowded a moment ago.

## Why this page has no example to practise

Like the Naked Quad, it never gets to be the chosen step. A hidden quad's
complement in a unit is a naked subset of the remaining squares, and for every
unit that survives to a hard position that complement is a pair or a triple —
cheaper rungs, taken first. Across the whole mined corpus a hidden quad was
available at zero of the positions that had exhausted the ten cheaper
techniques. So this page explains and stops there rather than inventing a
position the solver would never actually choose it in.
