Double Pairs with the counting requirement dropped — the same chute argument,
harder to see because the shape is untidy.

## The rule

Take a band or a stack and a digit, and look at two of its three boxes. Forget
how many candidates each box has and where they sit within it. Ask only this:
across those two boxes together, do *all* the places for the digit fall on just
**two** of the chute's three lines? If so, the two boxes must take one of those
lines each — every box needs the digit, and two boxes of a chute can never share
a line — so both lines are used up. The third box is pushed onto the remaining
line, and the digit is struck from its squares on the other two.

## A worked example

In the left stack, the 5s of the top and middle boxes appear only in columns 1
and 2 — three candidates in one box, two in the other, several sharing a column.
Columns 1 and 2 are therefore taken by those two boxes, so the bottom box must
put its 5 in column 3, and its candidate 5s in column 1 can go.

## It already includes box/line reduction

The deduction usually called box/line reduction — "the 5 of row 3 only appears
inside box 1, so remove it from the rest of box 1" — is this same pattern viewed
from the other end, and it needs no separate rung. The exact Double Pairs shape
is excluded here, because that cheaper rung takes it first.
